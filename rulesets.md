## Nine Ball
Nine ball is a common billiards game that uses the first nine numbered balls, with the nine ball being the titular money ball. The numbered balls are racked randomly in a diamond, except that there is a couple of restrictions:
* The 9-ball, being the money ball, must be placed in the center of the rack
* The 1-ball is on the foot spot

There is only ever one legal object ball at a time. The ball that must be hit first on each shot is always the *lowest numbered* ball in play. The balls need not be pocketed in order, including the nine ball. This means the game could end as early as the break shot.

### Victory Conditions
A player wins the game if any of the following are satisfied:
* If they pocket the nine ball in a legal, non-foul shot. (If the nine ball is pocketed in a fouled shot, then it is spotted instead)
* If they are the last player standing (should their opponents be eliminated via the foul combo)

### Ten Ball
Ten Ball is a common variation of Nine Ball. It plays very similiarly, but with the following differences compared to Nine Ball:
* A tenth ball (the 10-ball) is available in the game, and is used as the money ball
* The ten balls are racked in a triangle, with the 10-ball in the center, and the 1-ball on the foot spot
* The ten ball is spotted if it is pocketed during the break shot

### Six Ball
Six Ball is an uncommon variation of Nine Ball. It plays nearly identically, only there are six object balls racked in a triangle.

### Seven Ball
Seven Ball is a rare variation of Nine Ball. It play similarly, only that the balls are arranged into a fixed hexagon formation. (7-ball in middle, 1-ball at foot spot, and the rest are placed clockwise from the 1-ball.)

It is also restricted to 2 players, because each player is assigned a long-side of pockets that they *must* pocket the 7-ball into, being the money ball. This decision is made by whoever goes second, just after the break shot. It is because this decision is not immediate, that pocketing the 7-ball on the break shot results in it becoming spotted.

Additionally, unlike the relatives, pocketing the 7-ball on a fouled shot automatically results in a loss.

### Solo Play
Nine Ball can be played solo. The objective is to complete the game in as few innings as possible. The player earns 100 points for each ball pocketed, and loses 1 point for each inning that passes, and an additional 3 points for each foul. If victory is achieved by pocketing the 9-ball legally, any object balls still on the table are scored as if they were pocketed. Maximum score is 900.

Ball-in-hands are not available after a foul, *except* after a scratch (and even then, is restricted to behind the head string).



## Fifteen Ball
Fifteen Ball is a modest-length billiards game. The objective is to reach a score of 61 points by pocketing high-value object balls. The titular 15-ball is a "soft" money ball, in that it does not guarantee victory. The only racking restriction is that the 15-ball must be on the foot spot.

For each object ball a player pockets on a non-foul shot, they score points equal to the value of the ball (1 - 15).

### Foul System
Fifteen Ball uses a unique foul system in that most fouls do not result in a ball-in-hand (except scratching still gives a ball-in-hand), and that the cue ball needs to hit a rail (in addition to 2 object balls) on a break shot to be considered a legal break (assuming no pocketed balls). Illegal breaks don't subtract points.

Fouls in Fifteen Ball subtracts three (3) points from the offending player's score, and cause object balls pocketed in the same shot to be spotted. Players that foul 3 shots in a row are eliminated.

### Victory Conditions
A player wins the game if any of the following are satisfied:
* If they bank 61 points after a legal shot
* If thye have the most points after the last object ball was pocketed, or by having pocketed the last object ball if a tie was achieved
* If they are the last player standing

### Rotation
Rotation is a variation of Fifteen Ball. It plays similiarly, but with the following differences compared to Fifteen Ball:
* The following four balls are on fixed spots: 1-ball on foot spot, 2-ball and 3-ball on the corners opposite the foot spot, and 15-ball in the center
* Like in Nine Ball, the first ball that must be hit is the lowest ball currently in play
* If a player fouls, the incoming player has the option to take an inning as normal, or to force the fouling player to shoot again. No points are deducted for any fouls
* If a scratch occurs, ball-in-hand must be placed behind the head string. This string must be crossed *before* a ball can be hit, or else another foul is incurred

### Solo Play
Fifteen Ball can also be played as a solo game. The objective is to pocket all 15 balls in as few innings as possible. The player loses 1 point for each inning that passes, and an additional 3 points for fouling. Maximum score is 120.



## Eight Ball
Eight ball is a common 2-player billiards game that uses all billiard balls. The name comes from the fact that the 8-ball is the money ball. The numbered balls are racked randomly in a triangle, except that there is a couple of restrictions:
* The 8-ball, being the money ball, must be placed in the center of the rack
* The two corners of the rack opposite the foot spot must contain billiard balls of opposite suits

At least 4 balls must hit the rails in order for a break shot to be legal (assuming no pocketed balls). If the 8-ball is pocketed during the break shot, then it is spotted before the next shot is taken. The breaker can accept this outcome, or request a rerack. If the cue ball is scratched, then the opponent makes these decisions instead, with ball in hand behind head string if they accept the outcome.

This is one of the few games that does not eliminate players for a foul combo.

### Suit assigning
No suits are assigned during the break shot. Afterward, if a player pockets billiard ball(s) without fouling, then they are assigned the suit belonging to the majority of the ball(s) pocketed.

If there is a tie, then the last ball pocketed is factored out when determining the majority. (For example: If the pocketed order is Solid-Stripe-Stripe-Solid, then the last Solid is excluded, and the player would be assigned Stripes.)

After suits are assigned, then each player is considered to have their own set of object balls that must be struck first in a shot. Only when a player has sunk the entirety of their suit does the 8-ball become legal to strike.

### Victory Conditions
Once the 8-ball is the final ball on the board for a given player, that player must choose a called pocket on subsequent shots.

The game ends if the 8-ball is pocketed after the break shot. Who wins, however, is dependant on the manner in which it was pocketed.
* A player that pockets the 8-ball legally and in the **correct** pocket, with no foul, wins that game
* A player that pockets the 8-ball before (or on the same shot as) they pocket their last object ball loses the game
* A player that pockets the 8-ball in an incorrect pocket loses the game
* A player that pockets the 8-ball and fouls in the same shot loses the game

### Solo Play
Eight Ball can also be played as a solo game. The objective is to pocket *one* suit of balls (assigned using the rules above), and then pocket the 8-ball in as few innings as possible. The player earns 200 points for each assigned ball pocketed; and loses 1 point for each inning that passes, 3 points for each foul, and 100 points for each rival ball pocketed. Maximum score is 1600.



## Cutthroat
Cutthroat is a 3-player billiards game. The objective is to pocket opposing object balls while keeping your own in the field. As all numbered balls are distributed evenly amongst the three groups, there is no money ball. Object balls are racked as follows:
* The 1-ball is placed on the foot spot
* The 6-ball and 11-ball are placed on the other corners of the rack (at random)
* All other balls placed randomly

Once a player is assigned a group, they can hit any ball that does not belong to their group as the first ball of a shot. Still, it is legal to extend a player's inning at the cost of one of their own object balls.

### Group handling
Object balls are divided into three groups. The "low" group is assigned to balls numbered 1-5, the "middle" group is assigned to balls numbered 6-10, and the "high" group is assigned to balls numbered 11-15.

Because group assignments can be exceptionally complex when done the "proper" way, this program simplifies this process sharply and randomly assigns groups to each of the players at the start of the game.

### Scoring and Victory
As a result of the way the scoring mechanics are inverted, a player will take no more innings if all of their object balls are pocketed. The last player that still has object ball(s) on the table wins. Foul combos play no role in this game.

In the event a player scratches, then one object ball from each opponent (if available) is spotted on the table in an unpredictable order. Furthermore, if there is a circumstance in which all object balls have been pocketed (leaving at most the cue ball), then one object ball from each player is spotted in random order. These mechanics allow a player previously "eliminated" to return to the table and continue playing.



## One Pocket
One-Pocket is a semi-long 2-player billiards game. The objective is to round up the majority of the balls into your own pocket and claim victory. One-Pocket is strictly a simple scoring game, as there is no money ball or racking restrictions.

After the balls have been racked, the breaker needs to choose a foot pocket before carrying out the break shot, as only object balls that land in these foot pockets on a legal shot are counted towards one player or the other. Object balls that are pocketed in any other matter are queued for spotting instead. The queue is emptied at end of the inning, or if there are no more object balls in play, whichever comes first.

### Foul System
One-Pocket uses a unique foul system in that most fouls do not result in a ball-in-hand (except scratching still gives a ball-in-hand behind head string). Also, the cue ball needs to hit a rail on the break shot (in addition to 2 object balls) to be considered a legal break (assuming no pockets). An illegal break simply counts as a normal foul for One-Pocket's purposes, there is no re-racking option.

Instead of granting opponent ball-in-hands, One-Pocket reverts player scores to what they were prior to the foul shot, and also deducts a point from the offending player's score. These deductions are made by queueing the illegally pocketed ball(s) plus an additional penalty for spotting. If such a deduction would cause a player's score to fall below zero, then it must be made up at a later time by queueing the scored ball(s) for spotting as listed above.

### Victory Conditions
A player wins the game if any of the following are satisfied:
* If they secure eight object balls in their designated pocket at the end of a legal shot
* If they are the last player standing



## Cribbage
Cribbage is a semi-long 2-player billiards game. The objective is to score a majority of the available "cribbage"s. The 15-ball is a soft money ball, subject to restrictions documented in its Scoring section. There are some major racking restrictions at the start of a game:
* The 15-ball is placed in the center of the rack
* None of the pairs of corner balls are able to add to 15

### Scoring
A cribbage is scored whenever a pair of object balls adds to 15. Whenever an object ball becomes pocketed, its "mate" must be pocketed on the very subsequent shot. If multiple such balls are pocketed, then at least one mate must be pocketed in each subsequent shot. If these objectives are missed, then a foul occurs.

The 15-ball itself is unable to be scored while there are other object balls on the table; pocketing the ball this way will result in a spot back on the table. However, if it is the final ball on the table, then it can be safely pocketed by itself to score a cribbage and end the game.

### Foul System
Cribbage uses a unique foul system. When a foul does occur, the incoming player has the option to simply take the table as is (assuming no scratches), or to have a ball-in-hand behind head string. Also, 4 object balls need to hit a rail on the break shot to be considered a legal break (assuming no pockets).

Either way, unpaired object balls are spotted back onto the table.

### Victory Conditions
A player wins the game if any of the following are satisfied:
* If they secure five cribbages
* If all object balls have been legally pocketed without either player securing five cribbages, the player who pockets the 15-ball legally instead wins the game
* If they are the last player standing

## Solo Play
Cribbage can also be played as a solo game. The objective is to secure all 8 cribbages (100 points each) in as few innings as possible. The player loses 1 point for each inning that passes, and an additional 3 points for fouling. Maximum score is 800.



## Straight Pool
Straight Pool is a long 2-player billiards game. The objective is to score a large number of object balls over the course of several racks. There is no money ball, but there are a few racking restrictions at the start of a game:
* The 1-ball is placed on the left rear corner (looking from the head of the table)
* The 5-ball is placed on the right rear corner

For each object ball a player pockets in a legal shot, they score one point.

### Foul System
Straight Pool uses a foul system akin to One Pocket in that most fouls do not result in a ball-in-hand (except scratching still gives a ball-in-hand behind head string). Also, the cue ball needs to hit a rail on a break shot (in addition to 2 object balls) to be considered a legal break (assuming no pockets). Illegal breaks subtract two (2) points from the offending player's score, and the incoming player has the option to accept the table, or force the offending player to rebreak.

Instead of granting opponent ball-in-hands, Straight Pool subtracts one (1) point from the offending player's score (in addition to nullifying pocketed balls). If a single player scores three fouls in succession, fifteen (15) points are subtracted in addition to normal penalties (but in place of elimination), and the foul combo is reset to zero.

### Subsequent racks
If, after any shot, there is no more than one object ball in play, pocketed balls become reracked right away. The fifteenth ball (assuming it has not been pocketed also) and/or the cue ball may be repositioned if necessary.

Score is cumulative from rack to rack. The game instead ends if a player secures 100 points after a legal shot, winning the game.

### Solo Play
Straight Pool can also be played as a solo game. The objective is to score as many points as possible, in a single inning. The score resets if the player fails to pocket an object ball in any shot, or if they foul their shot. Maximum score is infinite, because fresh racks do not break a streak; only the conclusion of an inning does.
